# Buck Shlegeris

## Summary

Buck Shlegeris is a researcher at METR (Machine Evaluation and Tooling Research), the organization developing the capability evaluations that would serve as the quasi-independent gatekeeper under proposed superintelligence regulations. He is part of the core team (Jacob Hilton, David Bergal, Ben Cottier, Ryan Kidd) building the evaluations that determine whether AI systems cross the danger threshold. On September 14, 2026, he responded to Kevin Bass's Anthropic Network audit with a single "K" — a dismissive burn that inadvertently amplified the audit to a wider audience.

## The Kevin Bass Exchange

When Kevin Bass published the Anthropic Network audit (September 14, 2026) — reconstructing the $166M NPT grant flow to METR's network, naming Series H investors (Jane Street, D.E. Shaw Ventures), and raising the structural conflict of METR evaluating Anthropic's models while funded by the same network — Shlegeris responded with:

> "if only there was a way to check how much of this is accurate instead of just assuming it's all correct"

The K (burn) was perceived as dismissive and evasive rather than substantive rebuttal — Bass's audit was based on IRS Form 990 public filings and SEC documents, which are directly verifiable. The response drew more attention to the audit and its findings.

## Role at METR

Shlegeris is one of the named researchers on METR's capability evaluation team. METR's evaluations are central to the proposed regulatory framework: under the superintelligence ban bills, labs must pass METR's evaluations before deploying frontier-scale AI. This positions METR researchers as the de facto regulators of the entire industry — written by people who have historically worked at or been funded by the labs being regulated.

## The Evaluator Position

METR's position in the proposed regulatory structure is analogous to the FDA:
- Labs submit models for evaluation before deployment at frontier scale
- METR assesses whether models have dangerous capabilities (e.g., "ability to disempower state authorities")
- Labs that fail cannot deploy at scale; labs that pass can proceed

The structural criticism: METR evaluates the products of companies whose investors fund METR. The burn response from Shlegeris — rather than addressing the specific numbers — was perceived as deflecting from the verifiable public data.
