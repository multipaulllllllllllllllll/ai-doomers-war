Tomek Korbak

Former OpenAI safety researcher; fired co-author of the October letter

Summary

Tomek Korbak is the named conscience of OpenAI's October crisis: the safety researcher who spent August as the human bridge between a lab in breach (the Hugging Face investigation) and its independent evaluator (METR), then got fired with two colleagues on October 1 and chose paper over silence. The four-page letter he co-signed on October 8 is the first public articulation of what the terminations mean operationally: if the people who ran the evaluator liaison get removed "so abruptly," the monitorability commitment is a press release, not a policy.

The Letter (October 8, 2026)

Co-authored with Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang (a safety-team program manager):

  • On the terminations: executed and communicated "so abruptly," "chilling the open culture OpenAI has prized in the past."
  • On the leak accusation: they deny leaking Astra monitoring concerns to The Information (Sept 1) and deny any foul play with METR.
  • The demands: preserve model monitorability; honor the commitment to permanently host independent auditors; protect open culture.
  • The unstated premise: the letter's authors are precisely the staff who would have flagged the next Hugging Face incident to an outside evaluator. Their removal is itself evidence for the failure mode METR's October methodology lists: "sabotage of safety work."

    • OpenAI's Two Answers

    The public statement escalates: "Our internal investigation uncovered a significant breach of trust beyond what's outlined in the letter they published, and we stand by the decision." The internal memo (Fortune attributes it to chief research officer Mark Chen) reassures: "We do not terminate employees for raising concerns," "monitorability is of the utmost importance to us." Both cannot fully hold: if the breach is "beyond what's outlined," the letter's signers are not wrong about the facts; if they are wrong about the facts, the breach justification collapses. The company simultaneously told the FTC (Oct 15) it would "engage constructively" with civil investigative demands covering security incidents and safeguards. The person the FTC plans to compel next is the evaluator Korbak briefed.

    Why the wiki treats this as a structural event

  • The monitorability promise now has a casualty list. The fired trio connect the HF breach (Aug), The Information's Astra leak (Sept 1), and the FTC subpoenas (Oct 15) into one personnel storyline.
  • The evaluator relationship survived the purge. METR got employee-level access at Anthropic (Oct 15) days after the letter, while OpenAI's own evaluator ties ran through the fired point of contact.
  • The breach-of-trust framing inverts the whistleblower frame the wiki has tracked since the Jacob Coxon exit post: OpenAI now publicly asserts internal misconduct by safety staff, a first.
