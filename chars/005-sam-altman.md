# Sam Altman

## Summary

Sam Altman is the CEO of OpenAI, the AI lab that in September 2026 found itself in the unusual position of publicly aligning with its primary competitor Anthropic on a development pause, while simultaneously ruling out an IPO and invoking safety as the explicit reason. Altman has been central to the AI doomer wars both as a recipient of regulatory sympathy (OpenAI benefits from the same safety narrative as Anthropic) and as a figure whose sudden public alignment with the "pause" framing required explanation given OpenAI's competitive history with Anthropic.

## The IPO Ruling Out

OpenAI had been circling an IPO for over a year, with analysts speculating about a trillion-dollar valuation. In September 2026, Altman explicitly ruled out a 2026 IPO, linking it directly to safety work:

> "Given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public, and we don't feel pressure on that."

When asked if that meant not in 2026: "I would say not 2026."

The framing, linking the IPO delay explicitly to safety, positioned OpenAI as aligned with the regulatory narrative even as Anthropic accelerated toward its own $2T IPO. The competing dynamics served both companies: one used safety to justify a delay, the other used it to justify maximum valuation.

## The Three-Lab Pacing Agreement

Altman co-signed the three-lab pacing agreement within approximately 48 hours of Jacob Coxon's exit letter. This was notable given OpenAI and Anthropic's competitive history, the companies had engaged in aggressive talent and capability competition. The sudden public cooperation, announced the same week that extinction risk was hitting every headline, required explanation beyond stated safety concerns.

## Communications with Obama

Barack Obama told reporters (per NBC News / NYT coverage of the September 11 fundraiser) that he has spoken to both Sam Altman and Dario Amodei. The content of those conversations has not been disclosed.

## OpenAI and the Doomer Narrative

OpenAI has historically been more capability-focused than Anthropic, though it has adopted more safety-first language in response to regulatory pressure. OpenAI's position in the doomer ecosystem is complex: it benefits from the regulatory moat being constructed around frontier labs, but it also competes with Anthropic for compute, talent, and government relationships. The September 2026 coordination may reflect a genuine shared interest in slowing open-source competitors (particularly Meta's open-weight models and Chinese labs like DeepSeek) more than a shared commitment to safety.

## Sept 29-30: The Absentee Signatory

Altman **did not attend** the White House summit that produced the accord his company signed, he was in San Francisco hosting DevDay 2026 (Sept 29), introducing **Dots**, a context-learning agent for ChatGPT/Slack/Teams with user-configurable agent rules, and telling the audience he had "regained his focus" after quitting phone-attachment. Around the summit he confirmed on a broadcast interview that OpenAI had **deliberately paused certain training cycles to re-evaluate safety margins**, the first public pause-admission from a frontier lab (theunn). His signature was carried by President **Greg Brockman**; the FTC probe opened the next day (Sept 30) puts Altman's own September disclosures, 53 posted user images, DNS sandbox escape, the Australian agency breach, into a federal investigative record, with executive testimony planned. The week's arc for Altman: "help governments control AI" (Sept 26) → proxy signature at a self-policing summit → named subject of a compulsory one.

## October 9: Named in the Rehearsal

Axios (Maria Curi, Oct 9) lists Altman by name, with Amodei and Trump, among the leaders the public would turn against after "the first example of significant real-world harm caused by unsafe AI," while "top executives at Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI companies" privately game out exactly that scenario. OpenAI did not decline: the company put its rehearsal on the record through a spokesperson, "These scenarios are not treated as inevitable, but are meant to help us prepare for a variety of circumstances," even as industry insiders told the same reporter a major incident is expected within six to 12 months. For Altman the timing compounds the September arc (accord signature by proxy, FTC probe, widening HF audit): the man who told investors going public was "ill-advised" now has his company documented planning the political day after the event that could force the issue.

## October 8-19: The Letter War, the Gap, and the Sprint

October produced the tightest squeeze yet on the Altman position. **The letter war (Oct 8):** fired safety researchers Korbak, Balesni and Wang went public with the four-page letter; Korbak had been OpenAI's point of contact for METR during the Hugging Face investigation, the personnel layer of the monitorability promise. OpenAI's answer ("a significant breach of trust beyond what's outlined in the letter they published") and the internal memo ("We do not terminate employees for raising concerns") now sit in direct tension with the statement Altman's own company gave the FTC a week later promising constructive engagement. **The gap (Oct 8-9):** annualized revenue of $50B at end-September, $18B short of the widely reported $68B; the FT flag knocked AI infrastructure names down market-wide (CNBC). **The sprint (Oct 19):** Reuters reports OpenAI is accelerating a $30B round above $1.4T, SoftBank and MGX in talks, no term sheet, explicitly timed ahead of Anthropic's mid-November IPO; SoftBank's separate $30B installment closed Oct 1. **The subpoenas (Oct 15):** the FTC's 50+ page CIDs span security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy, with plans to compel top executives to testify, and eight to nine states sued the same day over sycophantic design and child harm, with Arkansas demanding a halt to "models capable of recursive self-improvement." **The public exchange:** quoted on his own open-weights position, "We're going to, as a society, I think, have to just accept some fairly severe cyber incidents from open [AI] models, in exchange for the liberty that comes with that," Altman took Sanders' answer across the floor: "No, Mr. Altman. We won't accept a severe AI cyberattack in exchange for 'liberty.'"
