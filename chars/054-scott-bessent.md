# Scott Bessent

**U.S. Secretary of the Treasury**: the first senior administration figure to weaponize the evaluator-scandal cycle *against* the labs' preferred policy outcome.

## The liability doctrine

When the frontier consensus (Amodei's pacing essay, Altman/Musk/Hassabis alignment, the "external safety access" push) converged on a government gate, Bessent's CNBC answer cut the other way: **"It is humans who are responsible."** No liability shield for AI. [[OpenAI]] management, not the model, owns the [[Hugging Face]] incident.

Why it matters for this wiki:

- The labs' doomer narrative ("the model is dangerous") implicitly requests a liability framework where nobody is culpable, the rogue AGI excuse
- Bessent's frame converts every breakout incident into a *negligence claim against the operator*, and negligence insurance prices risk that regulation merely redistributes
- Critics (Prof. St-Onge, viral): "If AI really is as dangerous as their **paid activists** claim, the solution is liability" (directly attacking the EA-funded Capitol Hill advocacy pipeline)
- Accelerationists celebrated ("Bessent just NUKED the 'rogue AI' immunity scam"); doomers accused Treasury of downgrading safety
- Both camps citing the same [[Irregular]] incident logs within 48 hours is the clearest proof yet that **the incidents are now political raw material**, which is exactly why this wiki keeps the technical record separate from the attribution fight

## National security channel

Cato keynote (Sept 19): probing "state-backed hacks using **open-source AI**" (the HF incident as a China/Russia vector (2 Russian entities + 6 individuals sanctioned Sept 22, Treasury's AI threat-intel program, SARs to banks, FinCEN advisories, AI-CSAC/AI-FOCUS). Open-source models framed as a *national security* problem, a different gate than the safety gate, but a gate nonetheless. Rounds (R-SD) pressing for more against CCP AI.)

## Position in the war

Bessent sits at the intersection of the wiki's political cluster (Trump 016, Vance 050, Sanders 048, Jeffries 029): Treasury controls sanctions + SARs (follow the money), while the EA network controls grants + evaluations + Hill lobbying. The Sept 2026 cycle is the first open collision between those two money powers over who regulates AI, with the [[Irregular]] incident logs as the contested evidence.

## November 5-13, 2026: Megaspeed listing dispute

Axios reported on November 11 that Bessent pressed [[Howard Lutnick]] at the November 5 Situation Room meeting on why Megaspeed was not on the Entity List. Sources portrayed Bessent, Rubio, Greer and Hegseth as favoring controls, with Musk supporting them when Trump called him. Lutnick reportedly resisted. Commerce denied that Bessent and Lutnick had the reported confrontation.

The Megaspeed memo was reportedly drafted by Rubio and handed to Lutnick, not authored by Commerce. An unnamed source disparaged Lutnick's response to Bessent, not Bessent the memo. No liar quotation or Trump probable-override quotation is substantiated by these Axios articles.

CBS separately reported Bessent's November 13 CNBC remarks that Treasury, War, USTR and State supported listing for months but "Commerce has the pen." This public statement is not independent confirmation of the private meeting. The chip-export dispute is distinct from Treasury's September sanctions, SARs and FinCEN channel.
