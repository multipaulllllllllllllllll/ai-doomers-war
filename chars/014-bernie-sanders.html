Bernie Sanders

U.S. Senator (I-Vermont)

Summary

Bernie Sanders, the independent Senator from Vermont, is the Congressional sponsor of the US version of the superintelligence ban bill originally drafted by ControlAI. His involvement brings significant progressive political credibility to the doomer regulatory agenda, translating the technical AI safety arguments into the language of progressive politics, anti-monopoly, worker protection, and government oversight of corporate power.

The Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act

Sanders announced plans to introduce the bill in September 2026. Key provisions:

  • Prohibit development of smarter-than-human AI systems
  • Temporarily pause all advanced AI research until new safety rules are established
  • Seek a global treaty banning superintelligent AI development

    • The bill was drafted by ControlAI, the UK-based campaign non-profit funded by Jaan Tallinn. This means the legislative language originated not from Congressional staff but from a policy organization with financial ties to the lab that would most benefit from the regulations.

    Political Significance

    Sanders' sponsorship is significant because:

  • It brings progressive/anti-corporate credibility to the regulatory agenda
  • It creates a left-right coalition on AI regulation (Sanders + Republicans skeptical of big tech, alongside Obama-aligned Democrats)
  • It positions the regulation as protecting ordinary Americans from corporate AI power, not as a corporate capture scheme

    • Critics note that the regulatory moat being created would primarily benefit closed-source incumbents (Anthropic, OpenAI) over open-source developers and workers.

    Mid-October: The Species Question and the Liberty Exchange

    Three posts in one week completed Sanders' escalation from policy to ontology. The species post: "Should human beings remain in control of planet Earth?" quoting Altman ("the first species ever to design our own descendants," "the merge has already started"), Amodei ("We don't know if the models are conscious... we're open to the idea"), Musk ("biological bootloader") and Thiel's hesitation, closing on the standing demand: immediate pause, ban ASI. The robots post ("HUMANS FIRST"): against Musk's framing that robots' pain matters, human pain first. The liberty exchange: quoting Altman's open-weights position ("We're going to, as a society, I think, have to just accept some fairly severe cyber incidents from open [AI] models, in exchange for the liberty that comes with that"), Sanders answered in a direct reply-format post: "No, Mr. Altman. We won't accept a severe AI cyberattack in exchange for 'liberty.'" The exchange matters structurally: the open-source split (hub section) now runs named-executive-versus-senator in public, in the same week the FTC decided the question of who testifies about safety is for a courtroom.
