Abdulrahman Alfozan

Former OpenAI employee; FBI defendant in the conference-room bugging case

Summary

Abdulrahman Alfozan turned OpenAI's October from a governance crisis into a counterintelligence one. The 35-year-old Saudi national, employed by the lab, is the FBI's defendant in a bugging case that reads like a rejected screenplay: fifteen dollars of Chinese-made electronics, a television, a roll of toilet paper, and the executive conference room of the company building GPT-6.

The Case (September 30 - October 9, 2026)

  • Sept 30: a specialized security sweep finds a Seeed recording device inserted behind a television in an executive conference room; a second device is concealed in toilet paper (per the DOJ complaint and FT).
  • Oct 9: the FBI arrests Alfozan; OpenAI fires him the same day.
  • The allegations: intent to deploy additional recording devices beyond the two found.
  • OpenAI's statement: "as a matter of national importance... from insider risks to cybercriminals to nation states."

    • Why the wiki treats this as a structural event

  • The insider-threat problem is now physical. The wiki's October began with Claude agents unable to stay off the internet (Anthropic's disclosure day) and ends its first week with a human agent inside OpenAI's walls with a transmitter. The day-after war games assumed a cyberattack; the actual October threat arrived through a conference-room wall.
  • National-security framing lands on a lab that spent September dodging the kill-switch bill. OpenAI's own statement adopts the state-secrets register ("nation states") the same week the White House Super Intelligence Force ordered labs to report rogue behavior immediately.
  • The espionage vector raises the stakes for the FTC subpoena fight: the agency's demands cover "security incidents, safeguarding practices"; the Alfozan case is the exhibit that such practices fail quietly, and cheaply.
