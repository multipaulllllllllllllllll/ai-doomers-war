Elon Musk

CEO, xAI / SpaceX / Tesla

Summary

Elon Musk represents the most publicly contradictory position in the AI doomer wars: he has called AI more dangerous than nuclear weapons, signed the Future of Life Institute pause letter in March 2023, and publicly endorsed the doomer framing about extinction risk, while simultaneously building xAI (Grok), competing directly with Anthropic and OpenAI, and deploying AI systems at scale through Tesla and SpaceX.

The Pause Letter

Musk was one of the original signers of the Future of Life Institute's March 2023 "Pause Giant AI Experiments" letter, which called for a 6-month moratorium on training runs beyond GPT-4 level. The letter was coordinated by FLI, which was early-funded by Dustin Moskovitz.

xAI and Competing Interests

Musk founded xAI in 2023 to compete in the frontier AI race. His Grok models are being deployed commercially. His doomer statements coexist uneasily with his commercial position, he benefits from regulatory uncertainty that slows his competitors while building aggressively himself.

Relationship to Trump

Musk has aligned with Trump's anti-regulatory position on AI. Both are skeptical of the doomer regulatory agenda, though for different reasons: Trump sees it as a hoax; Musk has endorsed the risk framing while commercializing AI as fast as possible.

October 29, 2026: SpaceX customer concentration

Bloomberg reported that Anthropic was SpaceX's unnamed Customer B, as relayed by TNW. The filing described that customer's 34.5% share of total quarterly revenue, all from SpaceX's AI segment; it did not name Anthropic. Customer identity is Bloomberg's source-based reporting, not a named filing disclosure. TNW also reported compute commitments of up to $120.5 billion through 2029 and cancellation rights from early 2027; those contractual figures are retained here as TNW's account, not independently verified SEC text.

Source: [TNW citing Bloomberg](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-spacex-largest-customer-customer-b).

November 5, reported November 11: Supporting Megaspeed controls

Axios reported Trump consulted Musk before approving action against companies suspected of diverting NVIDIA chips on November 5. Musk backed Rubio, Bessent, Greer and Hegseth's case for controls; sources portrayed Lutnick as resisting. Commerce denied the reported Bessent-Lutnick confrontation. These are reported private deliberations, not an implemented export order or completed investigation.

Sources: [Axios Oval Office](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/trump-lutnick-nvidia-china-chip-export-controls); [Axios Situation Room](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/bessent-lutnick-megaspeed-nvidia-china-chips).

November 18, 2026: DealBook defense of Huang

At the New York Times DealBook summit, Musk compared removing Huang from NVIDIA to kicking Michael Jordan off a team. TechCrunch covered the comments November 18; CNBC's follow-up was November 19. Supporting controls on suspected diversion and defending Huang's leadership are distinct positions. NVIDIA reinstated Huang November 23 while its investigation continued.

Sources: [TechCrunch, November 18](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/elon-musk-says-ousting-jensen-huang-is-like-kicking-michael-jordan-off-your-team/); [CNBC, November 19](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/elon-musk-jensen-huang-midterms.html); [NVIDIA, November 23](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director).

November 19 disclosure: Tesla (Shanghai) approval, not shipment

The BIS table publicly released by HFAC lists a March 16, 2026 NVIDIA application for 4,000 chips for Tesla (Shanghai), marked "Approved, not shipped" as of May 11. The same submission date has BYD, 4,000, and Great Wall Motor, 4,800, with that status. This is not evidence Tesla received the chips, and the disclosed table predates the November 5 controls discussion.

Sources: [HFAC summary, November 19](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11.19.26-ECRA-Information-Disclosure-HFAC-Summary.pdf); [BIS table as of May 11](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11.19.26-ECRA-Information-Disclosure-of-BIS-License-Decisions.pdf).

November 20, 2026: Himes requests Starshield explanation

House Intelligence ranking member Jim Himes wrote to Musk requesting a senior SpaceX briefing on October Starshield disruptions in Ukraine. He asked what changed, why, and whether SpaceX or a U.S. government official authorized it. He said difficulty obtaining answers made it hard to continue supporting authorization of funding for SpaceX-developed government-owned systems. This was a request and warning about future funding, not a canceled contract or a determination that Musk acted unilaterally.

Source: [Himes letter, November 20](https://democrats-intelligence.house.gov/uploadedfiles/11.20.12_letter_from_hpsci_ranking_member_jim_himes_to_musk.pdf).
