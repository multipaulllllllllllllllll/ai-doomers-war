Howard Lutnick

U.S. Secretary of Commerce

Summary

Howard Lutnick is the U.S. Secretary of Commerce. His department houses BIS and administers export controls. He was the reported recipient, not author, of Rubio's Megaspeed memo. Axios portrays him as resisting controls advocated by Rubio and Bessent; Commerce denies the reported confrontation. That record does not support describing Lutnick as a China-hawk ally in this dispute.

Sources: [Axios, November 10](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/10/lutnick-nvidia-jensen-huang-china-memo); [Axios, Situation Room, November 11](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/bessent-lutnick-megaspeed-nvidia-china-chips); [Axios, Oval Office, November 11](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/trump-lutnick-nvidia-china-chip-export-controls).

May-November 11, 2026: Warnings and reported Entity List dispute

Axios reported on November 10 that Lutnick received a classified warning in May about Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence suspected of being a Chinese agent. Rubio oversaw the drafting, according to the account relayed by Axios from the New York Times. Separately, Axios's November 11 account describes a Rubio-drafted memo outlining concerns about Megaspeed, handed to Lutnick. The retrieved reporting does not establish that these were identical documents.

According to Axios's anonymous-source reporting, at the November 5 Situation Room meeting Scott Bessent pressed Lutnick about Megaspeed's absence from the Entity List, and Lutnick said he would look into it. An unnamed source disparaged Lutnick's response; that was not Bessent's characterization of a memo. Commerce denied that the confrontation occurred.

Axios separately reported that Rubio, Bessent, Greer and Hegseth supported controls; Trump consulted Elon Musk, who supported their case. Lutnick reportedly warned that action would start a trade war with China, and proposed further options after Trump approved action. This is attributed reporting about resistance to controls, not evidence that Lutnick authored a hawkish memo. The reports do not substantiate a Bessent liar quotation or a presidential probable-override quotation.

Sources: [Axios, November 10](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/10/lutnick-nvidia-jensen-huang-china-memo); [Axios, Situation Room, November 11](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/bessent-lutnick-megaspeed-nvidia-china-chips); [Axios, Oval Office, November 11](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/11/trump-lutnick-nvidia-china-chip-export-controls).

November 19, 2026: BIS remote-access rule

Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule addressing remote access to American super-intelligence technology (BIS, Nov. 19, 2026). U.S. News, citing Reuters sources, reported that tsmc then stopped taking 16-nanometer-and-below orders from unlicensed Chinese designers. Samsung's role in that tightening is single-outlet and is not treated as independently confirmed here. The rule is a Commerce product; this does not establish an outcome of the separate reported Megaspeed dispute.

November 13-23, 2026: Agency account, testimony and criticism

CBS reported that Lutnick agreed to testify before Senate Banking on December 10. This is a scheduled appearance, not completed testimony. CBS also reported Bessent's November 13 CNBC explanation that Treasury, War, USTR and State had favored Entity List action for months, but "Commerce has the pen." Bessent declined to discuss reported personal tensions.

The DOJ/Commerce joint statement is dated November 17, not November 16, and was updated November 18. It credits Commerce under Lutnick: an investigation began in 2024, NVIDIA records were requested in May 2025, NVIDIA put Megaspeed on its no-sell list in June 2025, and BIS conducted a June end-use check with Singaporean authorities and interviewed Alice Huang. DOJ then took the criminal lead. These are the agencies' account of an ongoing investigation, not convictions or a clearance of any party.

Cotton's October 13, 2025 published letter documents his warning to Lutnick about diversion risks and Megaspeed. The Hill's November 20 report attributes to Cotton the allegation that Lutnick ignored repeated warnings. Criticism and demands for investigation are not findings of wrongdoing.

NBC's November 23 report quotes Hakeem Jeffries calling for an investigation into business malfeasance by NVIDIA and alleged corrupt abuse of power by Lutnick and Commerce. Those are Jeffries' allegations and proposed oversight, not findings. Source: [NBC, November 23](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/congress-criticism-jensen-huang-nvidia-reinstated-ceo-trump-rcna610926).

What to Watch

December 10 testimony, the ongoing DOJ investigation, and any documented Entity List decision remain follow-up items. Export approvals in the May 11 BIS table are not evidence of shipment.
