# Jack Clark

## Summary

[[Jack Clark]] is a co-founder of [[anthropic]]. The company leadership page lists him as Head of Public Benefit and says he leads The [[Anthropic]] Institute ([[Anthropic]], leadership page, retrieved 2026-11-21). He is not a [[U.S. Department of Commerce]] official. His Commerce ties are policy: export controls, the Commerce Framework for AI Diffusion, CAISI inside NIST, and the June 2026 BIS restricted-access letter aimed at [[Anthropic]] models. Before [[Anthropic]] he was Policy Director at [[openai]]. He writes the Import AI newsletter.

He sits on the Founder LLC stockholder-vote lock with the other [[Anthropic]] co-founders, and the S-1/A names him as an initial manager of the [[Anthropic]] Public Benefit Fund with [[dario-amodei]], [[daniela-amodei]], and Dr. Neil "Buddy" Shah. That fund is not the Founder LLC. This sheet does not assign him an ownership percentage.

## Head of Public Benefit and The [[Anthropic]] Institute

[[Anthropic]]'s Institute post is dated March 11, 2026 on the live page JSON-LD (`datePublished` 2026-03-11T00:00:00.000Z; `dateModified` 2026-10-14T16:27:17.000Z). The post says the Institute "is led by our co-founder Jack Clark, who will assume a new role as [[Anthropic]]'s Head of Public Benefit" ([[Anthropic]], March 11, 2026). Sarah Heck is named Head of Public Policy in the same post. Policy priorities named next to that block include export controls.

His personal site still says "I'm the co-founder and Head of Policy of [[Anthropic]]" (jack-clark.net/about, retrieved 2026-11-21). That matches the title on his June 25, 2025 House testimony. It is stale against the current leadership page. Treat Head of Policy as the 2025 title; treat Head of Public Benefit as the current one.

The [[Anthropic]] company page on this wiki previously dated the Institute post October 20, 2026. That dating is wrong. The live JSON-LD publish date is March 11, 2026.

## Commerce, export controls, and CAISI

Clark's Commerce file is a lab-to-department counterpart, not a job at [[U.S. Department of Commerce]].

On June 25, 2025 he testified as Co-Founder and Head of Policy before the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party. The statement recommends that Congress "strengthen export controls of advanced semiconductors to China" and "invest in the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) within NIST" (House Select Committee on the CCP, June 25, 2025). CAISI sits inside NIST, which sits inside Commerce.

[[Anthropic]]'s position on the Commerce Framework for AI Diffusion is signed through Import AI 397 (24 Feb 2025), Jack Clark ([[Anthropic]], diffusion-rule position). That is direct commentary on a Commerce rule.

CSIS reports that on June 12, 2026 the Bureau of Industry and Security at Commerce sent a letter to [[Anthropic]] on restricted access to the company's latest models (CSIS, June 2026). That is a BIS action against the lab. Do not upgrade Clark to BIS staff.

The Seattle Times quotes him, as co-founder and head of public benefit, saying the company has "daily discussions with the Trump administration" (Seattle Times, retrieved 2026-11-21). TechCrunch (April 14, 2026) reports he confirmed, at the Semafor World Economy Summit, that [[Anthropic]] briefed the Trump administration on Mythos: "absolutely, we talked to them about Mythos" (TechCrunch, April 14, 2026). The political counterpart on the Commerce side of this wiki is Secretary [[howard-lutnick]]; the White House counterpart is [[donald-trump]].

## Restricted access and Mythos

[[Anthropic]]'s Fable/Mythos access note says that following a U.S. government directive, the company is restricting access to generally available models for users in a small number of countries ([[Anthropic]], Fable/Mythos access). Pair that newsroom line with the June 12, 2026 BIS letter. This sheet does not invent the country list.
