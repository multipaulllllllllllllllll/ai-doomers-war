TSMC

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company; foundry that halted 16nm-and-below orders from unlicensed Chinese designers after the Nov. 19 BIS remote-access rule

Summary

TSMC is the foundry in the November 19, 2026 remote-access story. After BIS issued a rule addressing remote access to American super-intelligence technology, Reuters sources told U.S. News that TSMC stopped 16-nanometer-and-below orders from unlicensed Chinese designers. The loophole, in that reporting, was Chinese firms designing at TSMC and reaching American AI stacks without an export license. This page records that claim as sourced, not as a TSMC press release. TSMC's own statement was not recovered this sweep.

November 19, 2026: 16-nanometer halt

The U.S. News piece (TSMC tightens China AI chip curbs as new U.S. rules close manufacturing loophole, sources say, Nov. 19, 2026) is the outlet of record here. It is a Reuters-sourced report. Samsung is mentioned in that same outlet as part of the tightening; no second outlet confirmed Samsung in the tenth-sweep cache, so Samsung stays flagged, not asserted.

The rule itself is a us-commerce / BIS product (BIS issues rule addressing remote access to American super-intelligence technology, Nov. 19, 2026). howard-lutnick is the secretary. President donald-trump is simultaneously talking Huang restoration with Xi (Reuters, Nov. 19, 2026). The foundry tightened on the same calendar day the President discussed the ousted CEO. Those two facts sit next to each other. They are not a single coordinated plan in the recovered sources.

Moonshot AI's reported scrap of a roughly $150 million B300 rental through an Indonesian cloud (The Information; paywalled) is a downstream customer story, not a TSMC filing. It lives on the NVIDIA sheet as single-outlet.
