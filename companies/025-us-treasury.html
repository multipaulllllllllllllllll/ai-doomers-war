U.S. Treasury (Bessent)

The financial state enters the AI argument - via sanctions, suspicious-activity reporting, and a liability doctrine that denies the labs their favorite excuse.

Two tools, one message

  • Liability, not immunity. Bessent on CNBC: "It is humans who are responsible" — OpenAI management owns the Hugging Face attack; no shield for a "rogue AI" defense. The labs' capability narrative, inverted: if the model really is dangerous, someone is negligent.
  • Sanctions, not safety gates. The HF/Irregular breakouts recast as "state-backed hacks using open-source AI": Sept 22 sanctions (2 Russian entities, 6 individuals), SARs to banks, FinCEN advisories, AI-CSAC/AI-FOCUS, G7 agenda. The gate proposed is a national-security border, not an evaluation regime.

    • Why it matters to the graph

  • Every actor in this wiki who funded evaluations (Coefficient/NPT → METR/Irregular) or demanded a gate (FLI, CSER, the pacing coalition) now faces a Treasury that routes through money, not metrics
  • The "paid activists" jab at EA's Capitol Hill expansion (Sept 16) is the first senior-official echo of the wiki's core funding-loop claim
  • A gate is still a gate: "open-source AI is a national security risk" serves the same moat function as "unsafe frontier models" — the political side just swaps who gets excluded
