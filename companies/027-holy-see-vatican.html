Holy See (Vatican)

Summary

The Vatican entered the AI doomer war in May–October 2026 as something none of the existing players are: a global moral authority with no incentive structure the labs can impugn. The Holy See's positions, from an actual encyclical plus a papal flight presser, map precisely onto the wiki's fault lines: power concentration (vs. the Nvidia/$2T-IPO economics), autonomous weapons ("practically beyond any human reach to govern"), algorithmic redlining, data colonialism, and a direct call for government regulation of AI companies. In September it became the war's loudest counter to Trump's "hoax" framing.

Institutional Moves

  • May 15/25, 2026, Magnifica Humanitas. First AI encyclical; signed on Rerum Novarum's 135th anniversary, deliberately equating the AI transition with the industrial revolution. First pope personally presenting an encyclical. Co-presenter: Anthropic's Christopher Olah (see 056-christopher-olah).
  • Vatican AI Commission: promotes dialogue/study/investigation "to find ways to make sure that AI does not get to a point of destroying humanity or removing humanity from the conversation"; has fielded inbound interest from tech figures since the encyclical, per Leo on the plane (Sept 28).
  • Fr. Brendan McGuire (Silicon Valley parish, Vatican-tech liaison): "Tech industry leaders see something in what they're developing that is concerning them, maybe even frightening them... it would be morally reprehensible for us to not partner with them."
  • Claude Constitution contributors include Bishop Paul Tighe (Dicastery for Culture & Education), a formal channel already opened between the lab and the Curia.

    • October 2-15: The Alliance With Artists, the Warning on Control

    The Vatican front widened from rebuttal to institution-building. Oct 2: addressing artists (and on X), Pope Leo XIV called for "an alliance with artists and cultural institutions to safeguard our humanity," drew the ontological line ("There is an ontological difference, even before an aesthetic one, between art and what a machine can generate through statistical calculation based on millions of images created by others. Algorithms lack the spark of humanity."), and warned in the age of search engines that "the great danger is no longer just censorship, but homogenization." The Axios report tied the moment to the NYT story on Anthropic conferring with religious scholars to infuse morals into Claude, the lab's ecclesial channel and the pope's cultural counter-offensive meeting in the same week. Oct 5: at the FAO in Geneva, Archbishop Ettore Balestrero (Permanent Observer) argued hunger "will not be ended by intelligence alone, be it human or artificial," citing Magnifica Humanitas: "technology is never neutral," and regulatory tools are a duty "to uphold justice and avoid misuse"; the Church offers "a compass," not technical solutions. Oct 6-11: five Indonesian Catholic universities ran a Magnifica Humanitas AI bootcamp for 22 communication students, the encyclical becoming curriculum. Oct 15: the pope warned against "loss of control" of AI (ABC). Oct 14: Jensen Huang answered the criticism: "We all care about the same thing," the first direct bridge attempt from the industry side. Sources: Vatican News (Oct 2, Oct 5, Oct 6-11); Axios (Oct 2); ABC Australia (Oct 15); Fortune (Oct 14); research/2026-10-20.md.
