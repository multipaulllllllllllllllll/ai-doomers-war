## Story 1: Axios scoop, "AI companies plot the day after"

- Source: Axios (Maria Curi), October 9, 2026. "Scoop: AI companies plot \"the day after\""
  https://www.axios.com/2026/10/09/ai-companies-day-after-major-attack
- Archived: https://archive.is/2026.10.09-181655/https://www.axios.com/2026/10/09/ai-companies-day-after-major-attack (capture 9 Oct 2026 18:16:55 UTC)
- Amplified on X by Axios editor-in-chief Jim VandeHei: scoop post adds the detail that planners "assume one in '27", i.e. a catastrophic event in 2027.

Core claims, per article body:
1. "Top executives at Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI companies are privately gaming out scenarios for a public and political revolt after a catastrophic AI event." (Axios, Oct 9)
2. Anticipated event type: "a large-scale event, most likely a cyberattack, that shuts down access to financial services, internet connectivity, or even power and water." (Axios, Oct 9)
3. Named blame targets: the first significant real-world harm from unsafe AI "would turn an already wary public further against the technology and its leaders, including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, as well as President Trump for his reluctance to regulate it." (Axios, Oct 9)
4. Why this differs from normal scenario planning: "many top AI researchers and executives believe a major incident is inevitable." (Axios, Oct 9, linking their own Sept 15 Amodei agents/botnet piece). Industry insiders told Axios they believe a major event will occur within the next six to 12 months.
5. Direct quotes of record:
   - OpenAI spokesperson: "OpenAI conducts preparedness exercises where teams discuss and work through a range of potential scenarios. These scenarios are not treated as inevitable, but are meant to help us prepare for a variety of circumstances." (Axios, Oct 9)
   - Anthropic declined to comment. (Axios, Oct 9)
6. Strategic posture (the "between the lines" section): the planning involves red-teaming worst-case scenarios and "focuses mainly on racing to educate members of Congress." "Company executives know regulation has no chance of passing right now, but still want to shape the legislation and policies U.S. leaders will turn to after a first catastrophic event." (Axios, Oct 9)
7. Political assumptions inside the war games: Democrats ascendant after the 2026 midterms will move fast to shut down AI but will face obstacles: an aging Congress out of touch with AI; a global economy "heavily intertwined with an AI infrastructure buildout" where slowing it hits a fragile economy; and "too many open-weight models can already be freely downloaded and used to cause harm", a problem most cybersecurity professionals see as solvable only by using AI against rogue AI. (Axios, Oct 9)
8. Policy menu referenced: Sanders superintelligence ban (Axios links the Sept 3 Ban ASI Act story), Warren advanced-AI pause (Axios, Sept 16), and a bipartisan required kill-switch bill on advanced AI from Reps. Lieu and Moran (lieu.house.gov press release). Experts quoted by Axios question the viability of switching off all AI systems.
9. Counterpoint in the piece: one Democratic aide argues Washington sets partisanship aside in real crises, citing COVID and the 2008 financial crisis cooperation. (Axios, Oct 9)

Wiki significance: this is the first on-record confirmation that the labs' catastrophic-event contingency planning is a lobbying strategy, not just safety engineering. The execs rehearse the scandal, then plan to arrive educated when Congress asks what to do. It also ties three previously separate wiki threads: the Sept 29 kill-switch push and Sept 20 OpenAI containment failure (the rogue-agent-swarm scenario Axios links from Oct 3), the Sanders/Casar Ban ASI axis, and the inevitability rhetoric documented in the Amodei sheet.

## Story 2: CrowdStrike ARTEX campaign against South Korean banks (the catalyst example)

Axios's "Zoom in" section cites an active real incident as the template for the catastrophic event. Verified directly against primary sources:

- Source: CrowdStrike Intelligence blog, "Unknown Threat Actor Uses AI-Driven ARTEX to Target South Korean Finance", October 7, 2026.
  https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/unknown-threat-actor-uses-artex-to-target-south-korean-finance/
- Corroborated: Reuters, "South Korean banks were likely hacked by a China-based...", October 8, 2026; Infosecurity Magazine, Oct 7-8; New York Times, Oct 8.

Facts:
1. Campaign active late September to early October 2026 against South Korean financial organizations; data exfiltrated. Reuters: at least nine South Korean banks targeted since late September; Shinhan Bank reported personal data of about 25,000 customers compromised; KB Kookmin Bank reported 119 customers leaked. (Reuters, Oct 8)
2. Tooling: ARTEX, an open-source agentic penetration-testing tool developed in China (published on GitHub this year by a Chinese security engineer with the handle Autumn), used as the assault framework. The ARTEX instance's primary LLM backend was DeepSeek v4.1-flash, supplemented with GLM-5.3 (Zhipu AI) and Grok 4.6 (xAI). The attack wrapper was Anthropic's Claude Code: exposed attacker-controlled open directories contained Claude Code session histories, ARTEX configs, and Claude memory files. (CrowdStrike, Oct 7)
3. At one bank the actor breached a loan progress inquiry service used by financial brokers; at another, an employee mobile work-support system. (CrowdStrike, Oct 7)
4. Attribution color: CrowdStrike assessed the actor as likely a Chinese speaker, financially motivated, likely based in Guangdong province, probably age 26. The actor leaked their own details inside a Claude session (Telegram handle, birth date, a resume request naming Maoming, Guangdong). One Claude session asked where threat actors typically sell Korean breach data and requested help finding Korean Telegram data-sale groups. (CrowdStrike, Oct 7; Reuters, Oct 8)
5. Response: South Korean police launched a probe the week of Oct 5; President Lee Jae-myung called for robust response measures (Reuters, Oct 4). Anthropic did not respond to Reuters for comment.
6. CrowdStrike framing: "This activity demonstrates how AI tooling can enable a financially motivated threat actor to conduct multiple intrusions within a short time span." (CrowdStrike, Oct 7)

Wiki significance: first publicly documented mass-casualty-of-data event where a frontier vendor's agent tooling (Claude Code) sat in the attacker's hands while Chinese open-weight models (DeepSeek, GLM) drove the autonomous component. It is the concrete instantiation of the "rogue agent swarm vs. bad actor with available models" blame-game scenario Axios says the labs are rehearsing, and it strengthens the open-weight-policy argument the labs are war-gaming: the models behind this attack are exactly the freely downloadable class Axios cites as uncontainable.
