## 1. Trump China visit and backing of Jensen Huang

CONFIRMED, multiple outlets.

President Trump traveled to Shenzhen for APEC and dined with Xi Jinping. In a Fox News interview with Jacqui Heinrich, broadcast Wednesday on The Ingraham Angle, Trump said he spoke with Xi about artificial intelligence and that Jensen Huang came up only briefly (Reuters, Nov. 18 dateline; URL dated 2026-11-19).

Verbatim, Trump: "We did not discuss it. I might have referred to it just briefly because it's such a tragedy. It's a tragedy. He's such a brilliant man." Asked whether Alice Huang's affair posed a national-security threat, Trump said "I haven't heard that" and "I haven't heard that she was a spy." On the dinner: "We spoke the whole evening, and we talked about that (AI)." Reporting by Kanishka Singh in Washington (Reuters, Nov. 18/19).

Newsweek coverage of the same China trip quotes Trump telling China to "take care of our geniuses," with the line "Some people can't be replaced" in the same cluster. Treat the Newsweek packaging as secondary to the Reuters interview transcript lines above (Newsweek, around the Shenzhen visit).

Los Angeles Times, dated Nov. 16, 2026 (the brief's 2026-11-12 LAT URL is the wrong date; the fetched story is Nov. 16, authors Hadriana Lowenkron and Kate Sullivan). Trump said he "would not mind" Huang returning as Nvidia CEO. Additional LAT lines: "A lot of people are asking Jensen; they think Jensen should go back. That certainly isn't a bad idea." On security risk: "It was not put at risk." On Alice Huang: "Nobody's proven that she's a spy. She may not be a spy." Palmer Luckey, on Nov. 18, said China is "thrilled to have Jensen out" and that knocking Huang out "makes America weaker," tying a roughly trillion-dollar Nvidia cap hit to the ouster (TechCrunch, Nov. 18). Luckey wants Huang reinstated. That is advocacy, not a government action.

## 2. Lutnick vs Bessent (Cabinet fight)

CONFIRMED as a reported fight. Commerce denies the Situation Room confrontation.

### 2a. Situation Room, post-midterms Thursday (Axios, Nov. 11)

Axios (Maria Curi, Marc Caputo) reports Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confronted Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over failure to place Nvidia customer Megaspeed on the Entity List. Bessent asked, "How come Megaspeed isn't on the entities list?" Lutnick replied, "I don't know. I'll look into it." A source called the reply "bullshit" because Lutnick had been handed a May memo drafted by National Security Adviser and Secretary of State Marco Rubio outlining problems with Megaspeed.

Commerce Undersecretary Jeff Kessler was "repeatedly warned" in deputy meetings and deferred: "That's a decision for the secretary." Sources told Axios Lutnick spent the last year "undoing" efforts to hold Megaspeed accountable over trade-war concerns. Commerce has not added a single company to the Entity List in more than a year (Axios review of the Federal Register).

Commerce denied the confrontation. Spokesperson Kristen Eichamer: "This is false. Secretary Bessent and Secretary Lutnick had no such conversation." Record both the scoop and the denial.

Axios also reports White House and intelligence officials pressed Lutnick on Megaspeed since fall 2025; NYT (paywalled here) reported Kessler stopped a Megaspeed investigation after Nvidia intervened; FT reported delay despite other agencies pushing a blacklist.

### 2b. Oval Office, same day (Axios, Nov. 11)

Separate Axios scoop (Caputo, Curi): Trump on that Thursday authorized a crackdown on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia AI chips to China "for the third time this year," after consulting Elon Musk. Prior approvals: May, August, and Nov. 5. Lutnick is described as the official who "repeatedly stood in the way."

In the Situation Room with Bessent, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, USTR Jamieson Greer, and Rubio, the group sided with Bessent. Lutnick muttered he needed to "check with the president." Around 5 p.m. they went to the Oval. Musk supported Rubio, Bessent, Greer, and Hegseth. Lutnick warned it "will start a trade war with China." A source: "Everyone argued back that China is already doing to us what Lutnick warned would happen." Trump: "Let's get it done." Outside the Oval, Lutnick said, "I'll develop some options and come back." A source: "Everyone was like: 'No, no, the decision's been made. Go fucking execute.'" Axios: "Well, it still hasn't happened." Assembled from six high-level sources. Commerce again denied the Bessent confrontation.

Catch-up context Axios restates: administration loosened H200 sales to China with a 25 percent tariff; Huang argued selling less-powerful chips keeps China dependent on the U.S.

### 2c. On-record Bessent vs Lutnick (AP)

AP (article `17582a5098a5fb4c8fe866a084a6e52b`): Bessent said Thursday he was frustrated by Commerce delays restricting a Singapore-based computing company (Megaspeed) accused of diverting Nvidia chips. Verbatim Bessent: "I blame Howard." Of Lutnick: "He has a conflict of interest." Bessent: "Commerce has the pen." Lutnick: "I don't have a conflict of interest. I've recused myself from anything involving Nvidia." Lutnick: "We're going to get this done. We're going to get Megaspeed listed." Bessent, separately, on volume controls: "I don't think we need any further restrictions on the H200s. The licensing process is working as intended."

Same AP cluster: Rubio letter to Lutnick; Sen. John Kennedy, "I don't think Howard should be in that job"; Sens. Ron Wyden and Rep. Richard Neal investigating Lutnick; Trump, "Howard's doing a great job. We're looking at all of this."; Kessler, "We follow the law and the president's direction." Rubio, on Megaspeed: "The Megaspeed situation is a textbook example of how the Chinese Communist Party uses commercial relationships as cover for intelligence operations."

AP companion (`88142455395552afe74caec3399a648b`) covers the same Bessent-Lutnick split on chip-export restrictions, including both license approvals and the listing fight. Use it as a second AP wire, not a new set of figures.

Split to record honestly: Bessent is hawkish on listing Megaspeed and on Lutnick's delay. Bessent is not hawkish on further H200 volume cuts. Do not collapse those into one "China hawk" stance.

## 4. House PRC open-weight report (today)

CONFIRMED, official House pages, Nov. 20, 2026.

Chairmen Andrew Garbarino (Homeland) and John Moolenaar (Select Committee on the CCP) released a joint investigative report urging removal of PRC open-weight models from federal systems and high-risk critical infrastructure (House Homeland press release, Nov. 20; Select Committee findings page).

Investigation findings as stated by the committees: PRC open-weight models in use at 47 federal civilian agencies; present in 23 of 34 high-risk critical infrastructure sectors. Named model families: DeepSeek; Alibaba's Qwen; Moonshot's Kimi; Zhipu's GLM; MiniMax; 01.AI's Yi; Baichuan; Shanghai AI Lab's InternLM; SenseTime's SenseNova; Tencent's Hunyuan.

Committee line: "There is no such thing as a civilian Chinese AI model." Fourteen recommendations. Sixty-day implementation clock aimed at GSA, OMB, CISA, and NIST. Do not invent the fourteen recommendation titles; this pass used the press/findings pages, not a full PDF annex.
