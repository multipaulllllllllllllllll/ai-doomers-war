## Story 1: Anthropic files an S-1 and prices a November IPO

1. Anthropic, PBC published "Anthropic files S-1 with the SEC for proposed initial public offering" on October 26, 2026 (anthropic.com). The company said it had filed a registration statement on Form S-1 relating to a proposed initial public offering of Class A common stock, and that it intends to list that stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT." As of that announcement, "the number of shares of Class A common stock to be offered and the price range for the proposed offering have not yet been determined." Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are named as prospectus contacts. The page also points to a related Anthropic announcement dated November 10, 2026: "Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed initial public offering."

2. CNBC (November 2, 2026), covering the prospectus, reported nine-month revenue of $33.24 billion, third-quarter revenue of $17 billion, and a third-quarter net loss of $23.29 billion. Those figures are CNBC's reading of the filing, not a second Anthropic blog post.

3. CNBC (November 10, 2026) reported an indicated range of $70 to $78 a share on 825 million shares, with pricing expected November 18 and trading November 19, proceeds of up to $64.35 billion, and a fully diluted valuation of about $1.405 trillion. Reuters (November 13, 2026) reported that investors were flocking to the deal, that the offering was about six times oversubscribed by Thursday, and described it as a roughly $64 billion deal, while asking whether Anthropic can hold its lead.

Two-source status: the S-1 fact is Anthropic primary plus CNBC and Reuters. ## Story 3: METR's high-access Anthropic R&D-acceleration assessment

1. METR published "Anthropic AI R&D acceleration assessment" dated November 5, 2026 (metr.org). METR says its staff ran a high-access evaluation at Anthropic from August 12 to October 29, 2026, with internal metrics on pretraining, reinforcement learning, training compute, internal benchmark scores, speedup analyses, forecasts, and staff interviews.

2. METR's stated high-level conclusions, given with explicit uncertainty: as of October 2026, Anthropic is likely improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate it did between March 2024 and October 2025; METR "would not be surprised if the true acceleration was closer to 1.3x or 3x the historical rate." METR says a majority of that acceleration today is likely attributable to Anthropic's extensive use of AI to do R&D, with significant uncertainty, and that other causes likely played a large role in late 2025.

3. Redaction: METR says Anthropic had rights to redact non-public information; Anthropic "redacted a majority of the evidence we found most informative for these conclusions," described by Anthropic as aimed at removing confidential information that could accelerate other AI developers. METR says the public therefore has to rely on METR's judgment more than would be ideal.

Two-source status: METR primary only. An AP URL and a The Verge URL were fetched as candidate second sources; the saved bodies were not the assessment (AP homepage chrome; Verge unrelated product page). Do not treat AP or The Verge as confirming METR's multipliers. The October 14-15 embedded-evaluator arrangement itself was already logged in the eighth sweep; this item is the November 5 acceleration writeup.
